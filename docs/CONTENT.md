# Content

Every collectible item in the manor: what it is, how many of it, what one costs to carry, where it
belongs, and where it starts. **This is a proposal for the author's review (2026-09-14)** — nothing
here is built yet except the twelve spoons. Once approved, it is the list Phase 4 builds, and the
resources under `resources/manor/` become the source of truth for every row.

## Decisions this list follows

- **A set is every copy of one item type.** A set can have one member: placing the one television
  completes the Television set. Every completed set grants +1 slot (`docs/VISION.md`).
- **The totals from `docs/PACING.md` hold:** 250 items, a three-hour run, no set over 12 minutes.
  One set per type makes that **55 sets**, so the slot ladder runs 1 -> 56 and the finale costs 56.
- **Counts are what a real family owns**, not what fills a set. The household is two adults, two
  children sharing the kids' room, and a dog. The US average family is 3.15 people; two children
  matches a house with a kids' room and a kids' bath. Where a family owns more than a set can hold
  (a home averages 114 books), the set is the copies that are out of place and the rest stay on
  the shelf as scenery.
- **One of anything the author said is one.** One television.
- **Scatter is mostly random, and about one start in ten is deliberately absurd.** The garden hose
  in the bathtub and the toaster in the attic are from `docs/VISION.md`.
- **The first sets are small and near the door.** Four sets start only in the entry hall and living
  room; nine more start only on the ground floor. Everything else can start anywhere on the property.

## What an item costs to carry

The cost is the higher of the item's weight tier and its bulk tier. The legal costs are 1, 2, 4 and 8
(`Balance.SLOT_COST_TIERS`).

| Cost | Weight | Bulk |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | up to 0.5 kg | held in the fingers or one closed hand: a spoon, keys, a toy car |
| 2 | up to 2 kg | fills a hand, or two of it would not fit in one hand: a dinner plate, a pair of sneakers, a cushion |
| 4 | up to 8 kg | needs both hands or an arm round it: a paint can, a deck cushion, a pool noodle |
| 8 | more than 8 kg | both arms and blocks the view: a television, a bicycle, a laundry basket, a coiled hose |

## What changes elsewhere

- **The HUD tracker is two faces now** (`ui/Hud.tscn`, checked by `dev/HudProbe.tscn` with these
  55 names): a short list always on, and every set and room while Tab is held.
- **Every home above implies a piece of furniture**, and every absurd spot names a fixture (fridge,
  oven, washer, grill, vise, trunk, diving board). All of them are built, and every absurd copy now
  starts on the fixture its line names (`dev/ContentImport.tscn -- --absurd`, 2026-09-16): on the
  anchor when the fixture moves, so the duck in the fridge's door bin swings with the door and the
  toaster rides the trunk's lid; inside the container when the spot is behind a door, like the sneakers
  in the oven; and in plan space otherwise. The kitchen had no fruit bowl for the goggles, so its
  one-bay west run carries one (`BaseRun`, `bowl`), built from the same `FruitBowl` the dining table
  stands in its middle.
- **Slots stop limiting whole sets after set 23.** After that, extra slots only help the
  player carry several sets in one trip. The run time barely depends on it, because searching is
  30 of every ~43 seconds per item. Whether the late game still feels like it is speeding up is
  something only a play test can tell.
- **Sets of 15 are limited by searching, not carrying.** The Book set is 10.1 minutes at 42 slots,
  7.5 of them spent searching. If search time measures longer than 30 s per item, the 15-member
  sets are the first to exceed 12 minutes.
